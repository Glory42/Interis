Reel / slate style on mobile

Same design rules as the desktop version, adapted for touch. Hover becomes swipe or long-press, side panels become bottom sheets, and the main action always stays within thumb reach. Shown at 390px in your null-log theme.

1 · Movie and TV detail page

  • The title, scores and the start of the description now fit on the first screen; today the title starts around 900px down. The poster shrinks to 84px and overlaps the letterboxed backdrop.
  • A sticky action bar at the bottom (Log, Watched, Like, List and your rocket score) replaces the five stacked buttons and the 208px rating panel.
  • Tapping the rocket score opens a short bottom sheet with a full-width launch track, big enough for half points on a phone. The page stays visible above it (poster, title, scores) behind only a light dark overlay, so you can see what you're rating. It saves when you let go, and has a shortcut to write a review.

2 · TV seasons and episodes

  • A Next up card sits at the top while you're watching, with one large "Mark watched" button. That's the action you take most on a phone, right after an episode ends.
  • Season rows keep the reel progress strip. Tapping a row opens that season on its own screen instead of expanding a 366-row list in place.
  • Episode rows stay quiet: a 26px tick (44px tap area), the still, the title (truncated with "…"), and small marks for what you've set (♥, rocket and score). A ⋯ button opens an episode sheet with the still as its header, Mark watched, the rocket track, Like, Review and Up to here. No swipe gestures to discover; it works the same with touch, keyboard and screen readers.
  • The seasons in this example are in progress (S02 at 28/50) to show the Next up card; your real Bleach data is complete.

3 · Feed

  • There's no right sidebar on phones, so Now showing becomes a scrolling poster strip at the top with outlined rank numbers. Your week becomes a single slate line.
  • Grouped cards fill the screen width: poster, rockets, the review as a pull quote. Several "liked a title" events collapse into one line.
  • The "Log a thought" box moves into a bottom tab bar with a central ✎ button. Logging is the app's core action, so it's always one tap away (optional; the top navbar can stay).
  • The time and "Your week" numbers are illustrative.
